Most of us keep notes in three or four places: a phone app, "Saved Messages" in Telegram, screenshots, a paper notebook. Writing a thought down is easy. Finding it a month later is the hard part. Here are habits that help, whatever app you use.

## 1. Capture now, sort later

A thought you plan to "remember and write down tonight" is almost always lost. Capture it the same minute, any way you can: typing, voice, forwarding it to yourself. Tidying up comes later, and it's a separate task you shouldn't mix with capturing.

## 2. One thought per note

A long note called "Misc" is a drawer where nothing can be found. Short notes on one topic are easier to find, easier to link and easier to delete when they go stale.

## 3. Write the context, not just the conclusion

"Go with plan B" means nothing a month later. "Call with Oleg on Sep 12: going with plan B because A has no export" says it all. Who, when, why: a few words that save half an hour later.

## 4. Use the words you'll search for

Think about how you'll look for this note. If everyone calls the project "Landing" and you write "website", add both. Good search understands synonyms, but project names and people's names are best written the way you say them.

## 5. Keep folders to a minimum

An elaborate folder system on day one is a sure way to stop taking notes two weeks later. Three to five broad folders (Work, Home, Travel) and tags for cross-cutting topics are enough. Search does the rest.

## 6. Keep a daily note

One note per day: tasks, small thoughts, meeting takeaways. You don't have to decide where something goes; it simply lands in today. Unfinished tasks roll over to tomorrow, and anything important can be moved into its own note later.

## 7. Ask questions instead of browsing

Instead of trying to remember which folder holds a decision, ask: "What did Oleg and I decide about the plan?" Search by meaning and an assistant that answers with links to your notes turn an archive into someone who remembers everything you wrote down.
